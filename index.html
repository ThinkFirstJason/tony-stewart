01 / The Story

From circles
to straight lines

They call him Smoke. The name dates back to his early sprint car days, when he'd cook the right-rear tire chasing every last tenth.

Stewart grew up in Columbus, Indiana. He came through USAC open-wheel racing, won the 1997 IndyCar title, and then put together one of the best careers NASCAR has seen: Cup championships in 2002, 2005 and 2011, two Brickyard 400 wins at his home track, and a place in the Hall of Fame.

After that came the hardest kind of racing to get right. Tony Stewart Racing went nitro in 2022, and Matt Hagan brought home a Funny Car title in only the second season. Then Smoke got in the car himself: a Top Fuel dragster making 11,000 horsepower and covering 1,000 feet in under four seconds.
